glossa
(01) — Why

Every movie is full of words you almost understood. Glossa catches them as they are spoken, explains them in a language that feels like home, and hands you the list when the credits roll. No flashcards to make. Just watch.

(02) — How it works01 / 04
Watch

Press play with
English subtitles.

Glossa quietly saves each subtitle line as it appears. A green REC badge on the icon shows it is listening. If subtitles are off, it turns English on for you and can keep them hidden.

Catch

The hard words
catch themselves.

Every line is checked against a list of everyday English. Common words, names, sounds and filler are skipped, so you only see the words worth learning. You choose how hard the list should be.

Pause & Pick

Pause. The subtitle
becomes yours.

Click one word, or drag across a phrase like "piece of cake" or the whole line. The meaning appears right there, with Hindi, Hinglish and a button to hear it. Press ★ to keep it.

Keep

Credits roll.
Your PDF is ready.

Your picks come first, then every hard word with its meaning and the exact line it came from, with a timestamp. It downloads by itself.

    (03) — Features

    Small details,
    big vocabulary.

    01

    Pause & Pick

    Select one word, a phrase or a whole line in the paused subtitle. Words get a meaning and similar words. Idioms like "give up" get their real meaning. Whole lines are translated to Hindi, with the hard words explained.

    ↗
    02

    Hindi + Hinglish

    Every meaning comes in Hindi script and in Roman letters, so it makes sense even if you don't read Devanagari.

    ↗
    03

    The real line

    Each word keeps the line it was spoken in, with a timestamp, so you remember the scene and not just a definition.

    ↗
    04

    Hear it said

    Tap Listen on any card to hear how the word or phrase is pronounced.

    ↗
    05

    Your level

    All hard words, medium and hard, or only the hardest. Easy words never fill up your list.

    ↗
    06

    Private by design

    No account and no server of ours. Subtitles stay in your browser. Only the words you look up go to free dictionary services.

    ↗
    (04) — The PDF

    One movie.
    One word list.

    Revise on the way to work, print it, or send it to a friend who is learning English too.

    • a.Your picks first. The words you saved during the movie lead the list.
    • b.Short meanings. One clear sense per word, written for learners where possible.
    • c.Similar words. Simpler words that mean the same thing.
    • d.Names left out. Character names and places are filtered automatically.

    (06) — Questions
    Does it need English subtitles?

    Yes. Words come only from English subtitles. If the movie has them, Glossa turns them on for you, and you can keep them hidden on screen.

    Does it work on phones or TVs?

    Not yet. It works on Netflix in a desktop browser.

    Is my watching history sent anywhere?

    No. Subtitle lines are kept in your browser's own storage. Only the words and phrases being looked up go to free services: Free Dictionary API, Wiktionary, MyMemory and Datamuse.

    Can I make the PDF before the movie ends?

    Yes. Open the Glossa popup and press "Create PDF now" for any recent movie. You can rename the headline there too.

    What does it cost?

    Nothing. It is free and open source under the MIT license.
