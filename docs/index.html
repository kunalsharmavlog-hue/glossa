The small things, done well.

Three ways to understand

Simple English first, then Hindi in Devanagari, then Hinglish in Roman letters, so the meaning lands even if you don't read Hindi script.

Idioms, not word salad

Pick "give up" or "piece of cake" and you get what the phrase really means, not a word-by-word guess.

Whole lines, translated

Drag across a full line and Glossa translates it to Hindi, with its hard words explained underneath.

Only the words worth learning

Everyday words, names, sounds and filler are skipped. Choose all hard words, medium and hard, or only the hardest.

Private by design

No account and no server. Subtitles stay in your browser. Only the word you look up goes to free dictionary services.

Free and open source

Every line of code is on GitHub under the MIT license.

Ready before the trailer ends.

Glossa isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet, so you add it yourself. It takes about a minute and works in Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc and Opera.

Good questions.

Is it really free?

Yes. Glossa is free and open source under the MIT license. There is no paid plan and no account.

Does it need English subtitles?

Yes. Words come only from English subtitles. If a film has them, Glossa switches them on for you, and you can keep them hidden on screen.

Does it work on my phone or TV?

Not yet. It works on Netflix in a desktop browser.

What goes over the internet?

Only the word or line you look up, to Free Dictionary API, Wiktionary, MyMemory and Datamuse. Your subtitles and watching history stay in your browser.

Can I make the PDF before the film ends?

Yes. Open the Glossa popup and press "Create PDF now" for any recent film. You can rename its headline there too.
